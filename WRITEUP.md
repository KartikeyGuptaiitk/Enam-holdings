# FINN covering note

FINN turns a portfolio's filing stream into a shortlist and lets an investor inspect the evidence behind the numbers. I built it as a mobile-first investor desk: Today filters disclosures by materiality, Ask compares management statements with later disclosures, and Watch lets the investor decide what deserves an interruption.

The prototype uses saved NSE announcements and end-of-day prices for 18 holdings, standardized financial tables and extracted annual-report and transcript passages. It runs without API keys. After an initial online visit and service-worker activation, its main routes and saved research work offline. Today includes time windows, a portfolio heat grid, category and company filters, pins and muting. Watch has adjustable price and volume thresholds, a historical preview and a locally persisted inbox with duplicate suppression and archive/undo.

Ask's flagship is numbers versus narrative for Infosys, Wipro and HCLTech: explicit numeric guidance sits beside later comparable disclosures, with both source passages one tap away. Other views show financial snapshots, guidance, cash conversion, quarterly trajectory, selected balance-sheet fields, business passages and a mechanical bull/bear comparison. Missing information stays unavailable. The additional views do not imply complete segment analysis or autonomous investment research.

The main trade-off was to spend complexity on evidence and investor workflow. Classification, priority scores, arithmetic, alerts and current promise-matching rules run in code. Model adapters exist, but the shipped snapshot makes no model calls; its measured model spend is zero. Provider orchestration and live arbitrary-ticker analysis on Vercel are not claimed as complete. Python ingestion generalizes by ticker where upstream sources are available, with an honest unavailable state when retrieval fails.

The final cloud build reports 51 passing unit tests, strict typechecking, lint and production build checks. Browser acceptance covers mobile and desktop layouts, saved preferences, citations, alert persistence and offline research. The repository includes screenshots, recorded sessions and reproducible QA scripts. Manual reconciliation against Infosys, TCS and Persistent filings records PAT scope differences and rounding uncertainty in derived EBITDA rather than claiming full verification.

I deliberately left out BSE fallback, external management biographies, web push, account synchronization and a scheduled live refresh. Promise scoring remains a limited statement-based heuristic. Next I would formalize period, currency and entity-scope alignment, expand the manually annotated financial test set, and add a provenance-checked refresh process. The product aim is a useful daily habit: a clear reason to look, and evidence close enough to challenge.
