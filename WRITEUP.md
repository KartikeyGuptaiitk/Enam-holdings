FINN turns a portfolio's filing stream into a shortlist, then lets an investor inspect the evidence behind the numbers. I built it as a mobile-first investor desk rather than a dashboard: Today filters disclosures by materiality, Ask puts management statements beside subsequent disclosures, and Watch lets the investor decide which changes deserve an interruption.

The working slice uses saved NSE announcements, prices, standardized financial tables and extracted source passages. It runs without API keys, and its three main routes can reload offline after the first successful visit. Today offers time windows, company and category filters, pinned filings and muted categories. Watch includes adjustable thresholds, a historical preview and a replayable inbox that persists locally and suppresses duplicate alerts. Ask offers financial snapshots, explicit guidance, cash conversion and numeric promises-versus-delivery comparisons for Infosys, Wipro and HCLTech.

The central decision was to spend complexity on grounding rather than generated prose. Classification, materiality scoring, financial arithmetic, alert thresholds and the current comparison rules run in code. Research cards open the exact saved source passage. Missing documents remain unavailable; an unsupported claim does not become a plausible answer. Model adapters and a routing table are present, but the shipped snapshot makes no model calls. Its model spend is zero, and I have not presented that as evidence that a live reasoning system is complete.

Cloud validation covered a clean Linux production build, strict TypeScript checking, lint and 47 unit tests, including 40 real filing examples. Browser checks exercised mobile and desktop layouts, citations, unknown-company states, alert persistence and offline route reload. I also compared the latest results for Infosys, TCS and Persistent against original saved filing passages. That review exposed distinctions worth preserving: group PAT versus profit attributable to shareholders, statutory versus adjusted measures, and uncertainty from deriving EBITDA using rounded standardized inputs. The UI does not claim full reconciliation.

The cuts are deliberate. There is no BSE fallback, external management biography enrichment, web push, account sync, full LLM orchestration or advanced business and balance-sheet modules. Live ingestion depends on upstream access and Python, so deployment serves snapshots rather than promising arbitrary serverless scraping. Promise matching remains a narrow heuristic; its score describes selected statements, not management integrity.

Next I would formalize period and entity-scope alignment, expand a manually annotated financial test set, split the initial research payload and add a scheduled refresh with provenance checks. The aim is a dependable daily habit: less noise, a clear reason to look, and evidence close enough to challenge.
